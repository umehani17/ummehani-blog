My Journey with Programming Fundamentals: How Dr. Bilal Ahmad Shaped My Understanding of Code

Posted on April 2026 1000+ words

Last semester, when I enrolled in Programming Fundamentals with Dr. Bilal Ahmad, I didn't know how this course would fundamentally change my perspective on coding. I came in thinking programming was just about learning syntax, memorizing loops, and writing functions that worked. What I discovered instead was that Dr. Bilal Ahmad teaches programming as a tool for solving real-world problems—problems that matter to people, to society, and to the future of technology. This realization transformed not just how I code, but how I think about my role as a computer engineer.

Before this course, I had basic knowledge of programming from online tutorials and school projects. But there's something different about learning from an expert like Dr. Bilal Ahmad, who doesn't just teach syntax—he teaches philosophy. Dr. Bilal Ahmad is renowned as an AI and Machine Learning expert, specializing in model training on real datasets. He brings this expertise into our Programming Fundamentals class, constantly pushing us to think about how the code we write could solve actual problems. He often references his work with medical datasets, explaining how high-precision data collection and accurate processing can save lives. This context made every assignment feel less like homework and more like preparation for meaningful work.

The course began with the basics—variables, data types, conditional statements, and loops. But Dr. Bilal Ahmad's approach was different from what I expected. In the first week, instead of just showing us how to write an if-else statement, he asked us: "What problem are you solving? Who benefits from this code?" It forced us to think critically about every line we wrote. I remember one assignment where we had to write a program to process patient data. Dr. Bilal Ahmad explained that in real medical AI applications, even small errors in data processing could lead to incorrect diagnoses. This wasn't just an academic exercise—it was a lesson in responsibility. That's when I understood why precision matters in programming.

One of the most valuable lessons came when we studied arrays and data structures. Dr. Bilal Ahmad explained how machine learning models depend on how data is organized and processed. He shared examples from his own research, showing us how he trains ML models on medical datasets. He talked about how a poorly organized database or inefficient data processing algorithm could waste computational resources and time. Suddenly, learning about arrays wasn't just about passing an exam—it was about building the foundation for ML applications that could help doctors detect diseases earlier. Dr. Bilal Ahmad's real-world examples made the theory stick in my mind in a way no textbook ever could.

However, the semester wasn't all smooth sailing. The last week of exams was extremely difficult. We had multiple assignments pending from Programming Fundamentals, along with quizzes from other courses like Digital Logic Design and Database Systems. I found myself staying up late, going to university early, and barely having time to eat properly. The pressure was immense, and I realized that I hadn't prepared consistently throughout the semester. I had procrastinated on assignments, thinking I could catch up later. This was a hard lesson learned the difficult way. I made mistakes, submitted work that wasn't my best, and felt stressed in a way that could have been avoided with better planning. But this struggle also taught me something important: time management is a programming skill too. Dr. Bilal Ahmad often mentioned how professional developers work in teams with deadlines, and how real projects require disciplined work over time—not last-minute cramming.

Through the assignments and projects in Dr. Bilal Ahmad's class, I learned to write cleaner code. We worked on problems that required thinking about edge cases, writing functions that could be reused, and documenting our work clearly. Dr. Bilal Ahmad emphasized that good programming is about writing code that other people (and your future self) can understand. He reviewed our assignments carefully and gave feedback that went beyond just "this works" or "this doesn't." He explained the reasoning behind best practices, connecting them back to how professional developers and ML engineers write code in real companies.

One project that really stood out was when we had to create a program that could validate and clean data. This seems simple on the surface, but Dr. Bilal Ahmad showed us that data cleaning is one of the most critical steps in any data science or ML project. He explained how he spends weeks cleaning medical datasets before training ML models on them. A dataset with errors or inconsistencies can produce a model that looks good on paper but fails in real life. This assignment made me see programming differently—not just as writing algorithms, but as being a careful steward of data that will be used to make important decisions.

By the end of the semester, I had gained not just technical skills but a mindset shift. I now see programming as a discipline that requires both technical knowledge and ethical responsibility. Dr. Bilal Ahmad's approach—emphasizing real-world applications, precision, and the impact of code on people's lives—has influenced how I approach every coding problem now. Whether it's a small script or a larger project, I ask myself: What problem does this solve? Is my code reliable? Could someone depend on this for something important?

Looking back, this course with Dr. Bilal Ahmad has been transformative. It taught me that a Computer Engineer isn't just someone who writes code—we're problem-solvers, thinkers, and builders of systems that can impact the world. Dr. Bilal Ahmad, with his expertise in AI and Machine Learning and his focus on real datasets and real-world applications, showed me what it means to be a responsible programmer.
